# Using Pulse within WhatsApp's rules

This is practical guidance, not legal advice. Check Meta's current [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://business.whatsapp.com/policy), [Commerce Policy](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/commerce-policy) and the [WhatsApp Business Platform terms](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/business-terms) yourself.

## The connection

Pulse connects only through **Meta's official WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API)**, with your own Meta app, number and access token. This is the way WhatsApp's terms allow businesses to send automated messages. (Pulse 1.x linked to WhatsApp like WhatsApp Web; that connection has been removed.)

Using the official platform does not make every message acceptable: Meta's policies still apply, Meta rates the quality of your number, and numbers that get many blocks or reports have their limits lowered or are restricted.

## Meta's main rules and how Pulse helps

| Rule | What Pulse does |
| --- | --- |
| Opt-in before messaging | Each contact has a consent record (source and date). Campaigns go only to opted-in contacts unless you switch that off. CSV import can read a consent column. Integrations cannot mark someone as opted in; the opt-in must be recorded in Pulse. With "Require opt-in" on, scheduled messages also need a recorded opt-in, even when the customer wrote recently. A customer whom Meta reports as having stopped marketing messages from businesses (error 131050) is left out of later campaigns; replies to their own messages still go. |
| 24-hour customer service window | Free text is only sent within 24 hours of the customer's last message; Pulse switches to the template half an hour early (after 23 hours 30 minutes), so a message paced or retried at the end does not reach Meta after the window closed. Messages typed in the inbox and the `send` command are refused outside it, with a plain explanation. The integration endpoint sends an approved template instead when one is given and the customer's opt-in is recorded. Campaigns and scheduled messages send the Meta-approved template you chose to customers outside the window, or skip them. Automatic replies answer a message that just arrived; one written more than 23 hours 30 minutes ago (delivered late, or replayed after a restart) is not answered. Without a recorded opt-in, campaigns use the same 23 hours 30 minutes. |
| Templates | Created and approved in WhatsApp Manager. In Pulse you enter the template name, language and which values (for example `{{first_name}}`) fill `{{1}}`, `{{2}}`..., plus the header value or header photo/video/document link and link-button endings when the template has them. A template with an empty value is not sent. |
| Honour opt-outs | STOP, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, QUIT, END, short requests in the customer's own words ("STOP please", "please stop messaging me", "remove me from your list"), "rok do", "band karo", "mat bhejo", "stop na", DND, "do not disturb" and similar mark the contact do-not-message immediately, even when typed with look-alike letters, on every way of sending. START or "shuru karo" opts a customer who opted out back in; from anyone else it is an ordinary message and records no consent. Meta does not keep the order of deliveries, so a STOP or START written before the customer's latest choice is not applied when it arrives late; every way to mark someone do-not-message (STOP, the contact form, the list, CSV import) keeps its time for this. Every STOP and START is recorded; the confirmation reply goes out at most 3 times an hour per customer, so alternating STOP and START cannot keep the bot replying. Switching off both opt-in and the 24-hour rule asks for confirmation, is logged, and keeps a warning on Home. |
| Quality rating and messaging limits | Home shows Meta's quality rating. A red rating counts as "at risk": campaigns are paused and blocked. Keep the Careful pace while Meta's daily limit for a new number grows. |
| Blocked numbers, test mode, practice mode, daily limit | Apply to every way of sending: automatic replies, the inbox, scheduled messages, campaigns, integrations, test messages and the `send` command. The daily limit counts the last 24 hours, including sends that were cut off or timed out and may have reached the customer. |
| Never send twice | Every message (automatic replies, the inbox, test messages, the `send` command, campaigns, scheduled messages and integrations) is recorded with a label before it is sent, and a send from the dashboard keeps going when the browser tab is closed. A send whose answer from Meta is lost is recorded as "may have been sent", counts towards the daily limit and is corrected when Meta reports it; the inbox, test messages and the `send` command say "may have been sent — check the chat before sending again". An automatic reply is saved to disk before it goes, so a message replayed after a crash is not answered twice. The integration endpoint answers HTTP 202 `{"status":"unknown"}` (do not retry) and refuses a repeated `idempotency_key` for 24 hours, also after a restart (the history keeps a hash of the key in the message's label); a repeat sent while the first request is still in progress gets HTTP 409 "in progress — retry later", and a key whose request certainly sent nothing can be used again. |
| Numbers from Pulse 2.0 | Pulse 2.0 added India's 91 to 10-digit WhatsApp numbers starting 6-9 from other countries (Singapore's 6591234567 became 916591234567). Pulse works out once when 2.1 first ran on this PC, from the activity log's start entries and the numbers 2.1.0 saved (2.1.0 recorded nothing, and 2.1.1 recorded its own first start, which made customers added under 2.1.0 look older); only contacts from before then can be such a copy, and a contact that may be from either version is never moved (its opt-out still applies to the real number). When such a customer writes, Pulse moves the contact (opt-out, tags, notes, messages and waiting scheduled or campaign messages) to the real number; consent recorded under the old number does not move. An opt-out recorded under the old form still applies to the real number (also when the real number already had its own contact, or the old contact was deleted), and the old form stays opted out in case it really is an Indian customer. A contact that staff typed in for the Indian number and that never wrote, or any contact added after the upgrade, is left as it is. Every number is stored exactly as WhatsApp reports it or as typed with its country code, and messages, opt-outs and consent are matched to exactly that number: a contact form, integration or CSV row for +65 9123 4567 never changes the contact 916591234567. A number typed without + is read as that stored number first; only otherwise does a 10-digit mobile starting 6-9 get India's 91, a shorter or other 10-digit number must be typed with its country code, and no number is ever matched by its last digits, so a send, scheduled message, export or erasure never reaches a look-alike customer. Erasing a customer also blocks and masks the old 91 form in backups and the activity log, erases the old 91-form contact and its messages when it is certainly that customer's record from 2.0, and otherwise tells the admin that a record under the 91 form exists and was kept (it may be a different, Indian customer). Erasure also deletes the backup copies (.bak) of the files it changed. |
| Reach a person | AGENT, HUMAN, PERSON, "talk to someone", "baat karni hai" or menu option 4 pause the bot in that chat and put it under *Needs attention*. A voice note, a photo without a caption or a document, which Pulse cannot read, also puts the chat there. |
| Do not spam | Daily limit, warm-up limit (50 a day), cooldown and per-customer reply cap, loop detection, randomised gaps and batch pauses, automatic pause after 5 failed sends or when several recipients opt out. |
| Accurate information | Default replies only use details you entered. A reply whose details are empty is never sent with a gap. |
| Protect personal data | Customer data is encrypted on disk; the access token and app secret are sealed with your Windows account; per-customer export and deletion; retention period. |

## Costs

Meta bills some messages (mostly templates) to your Meta account; prices depend on the category and the customer's country. Check WhatsApp Manager for current prices.

## Content

Do not send messages about illegal goods, gambling, adult content, weapons, drugs, deceptive offers, or anything Meta's Commerce and Business Messaging policies prohibit. Do not ask customers for card numbers, passwords or one-time codes.
